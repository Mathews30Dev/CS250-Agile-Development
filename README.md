# CS250-Agile-Development

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS250 at SNHU. Throughout this course, I explored the Scrum-Agile framework and the different roles involved in software development, including the Scrum Master, Product Owner, developer, and tester.

The SNHU Travel project allowed me to understand how Agile teams organize their work, communicate with stakeholders, and adapt to changing requirements. This final retrospective reflects on those experiences and the lessons I learned throughout the development process.

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

Understanding user needs starts with communicating with users and stakeholders to identify what they expect from the software. User stories help translate those needs into smaller, manageable features that developers can work on. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned how user stories could be used to describe features such as vacation preferences, hot deals, and price limits. They also helped testers create test cases to verify that the software meets the expected requirements. I believe user stories are important because they help the development team understand not only what needs to be built but also why the feature matters to the user.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing a program, I prefer breaking larger problems into smaller tasks and working on them one at a time. This makes the development process easier to manage and allows me to identify problems before they become more difficult to fix. One Agile practice I would like to incorporate into my future projects is using a task board to organize and track progress. I would also like to use regular feedback, testing, and retrospectives to identify areas for improvement. The SNHU Travel project showed me how important it is to remain flexible when requirements change and to communicate with others before making major changes.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

I believe being a good team member means communicating clearly, being responsible for your work, and being willing to help others when needed. Software development involves different roles working toward the same goal, so collaboration is essential. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned how important communication between the Product Owner, developer, and tester was when requirements changed. Asking questions, sharing progress, and providing feedback can help prevent misunderstandings and unnecessary work. A good team member should also respect other people's ideas and be open to learning from their experiences.
